# ISM decoder (433 / 868 MHz)

The **ISM** key in DECODE decodes devices on 433 and 868 MHz, such as weather sensors, remote controls and tyre sensors, via rtl_433.

ISM (433 / 868 MHz devices such as weather sensors and remote controls, decoded by [rtl_433](https://github.com/merbanan/rtl_433)): buttons 433.92 and 868.30 MHz centre the VFO, which runs at 2.048 MS/s (see Channels below). The audio of the VFO is muted, and REC records nothing. The table shows the device, ID, channel, level and data; a message the device repeats is one row with ×N. **ISM** again, **OFF** in the card or any band button ends it and restores the sample rate. While ISM is on, RTL2832 AGC and tuner AGC are on (AGC+ and Remove DC peak off); at the end the gain, RTL AGC, AGC+ and Remove DC peak from before come back. ISM turns ACARS, VDL2 and DAB off. RTL-SDR only; not on LimeSDR, Airspy or a chained VFO.

**Channels.** rtl_433 gets narrow channels of 256 kS/s that RADIMCOM cuts out of the 2.048 MS/s of the dongle, one rtl_433 each: 433.92 and 433.42 MHz at the 433.92 preset, 868.30 and 868.95 MHz (Wireless M-Bus meters, mostly encrypted) at the 868.30 preset with rtl_433's automatic level (hover the message counter). With the whole 2 MHz at once, noise and several transmitters merge into long packages and rtl_433 decodes far fewer messages (on a 5 min test recording 107 instead of 236). A device further than about ±110 kHz from a channel centre is not decoded.

**RAW** shows packages of at least 20 pulses that no decoder read, as grey rows: modulation guess, pulses, length and the demodulated bits from rtl_433's pulse analyzer; the tooltip has the flex decoder hint (`-X ...`) and **⧉** shows the pulses at triq.org/pdv. Many are noise or devices rtl_433 does not know. Packages of the same kind within ±15 kHz in 10 s are one row with ×N. RAW rows are not written to the log.

**Views.** **MESSAGES** is the table of messages (newest activity on top; category icon, signal bars, ✓ when the device's own check — CRC or parity — passed; a new or repeated message flashes its row). **DEVICES** shows a card per device (model + ID + channel): its latest values in large digits (temperature, humidity, wind with a compass; pressure, rain, UV, light, power and the other fields below), a 24 h curve of temperature (yellow) and humidity (dashed blue), battery (red and blinking when LOW), signal bars, the age of its last message, its typical interval (↻) and message count. A card fades when the device has been quiet for 3 of its intervals (at least 5 min) and turns grey after 30 min; a new message lights it up. All cards are equally high: the other fields are one line (all of them in its tooltip), and a device without temperature or humidity shows its messages as ticks (1 h) or as bars per 5 minutes (6 h / 24 h) in place of the curve. **TIMELINE** has, at 1 h, a dot for each message of each device; at 6 h and 24 h a block for each 5 minutes with messages (brighter = more messages, the count in its tooltip); and the device's interval. **1 h / 6 h / 24 h** in the header (shown in DEVICES, TIMELINE and the device tab, remembered in the browser) sets the time shown by the card curves and activity, the timeline and the device charts; a click on a name opens the device's tab. The server keeps up to 300 devices (not over a restart; CLR clears them too).

**Device tab.** A click on a card opens a tab of that device beside MESSAGES / DEVICES / TIMELINE (its name; ✕ closes it). On top its current values, model and rtl_433 protocol, ID / channel, frequency and modulation, signal, when it was heard first and last, message count and interval, message check, battery, and ✎ ☆ ⊘. Below, over **1 h / 6 h / 24 h**: a chart for every number the device sends (temperature, humidity, wind average with gusts, wind direction as dots, pressure, rain, UV, light, power, energy, battery voltage; other numbers such as switch or button IDs, codes and rolling counters are shown as *Values* in the head of the tab, not as charts, and a device of the category 📡 Other gets no field charts at all), **Signal** (RSSI and dashed SNR) and **Messages per 1 / 5 / 15 min**. Each chart has its latest value, min, average and max. The cursor moves in all charts at once and the legend under each chart shows the values at that time (UTC); **drag over a chart to zoom all charts in**, double click to go back to the whole range; a click on a legend entry hides that curve. New messages update the charts and keep the zoom; a silence longer than 4 intervals (at least 10 min) breaks the line. The server keeps one point per minute for 24 h (the last values in the minute), refreshed with each message of the device.

**Names, pins, hidden devices.** ✎ gives a device a name, ☆ pins it to the top of DEVICES, ⊘ hides it everywhere (**HIDDEN** shows hidden devices again). They are kept for the whole station in `data\ism_devices.json` (all VFOs). **NEW** marks for 10 min a device not heard on this PC in the last 60 days (`data\ism_known.json`).

**Categories and filter.** Each device gets a category from its model and fields: 🌡 weather, 🚗 TPMS (tyres), 🔑 remotes, 🚪 security (doors, motion), 🔥 alarms, ⚡ meters, 📡 other, ❓ RAW. The chips with their counts show / hide a category; with the filter field they apply to all three views. Right of them: messages per minute in the last hour, number of devices and the three models with the most messages.

A Wireless M-Bus meter is one row for an hour with its latest message. The same message of a device within 1 min is one row with ×N, moved to the top with the time of the last one. One packet read by two decoders of rtl_433 is one row with both device names (e.g. *LaCrosse-TX29IT / LaCrosse-TX35DTHIT*). Units are SI (°C, km/h, mm).

rtl_433 is a separate program (`lib\ism\rtl_433.exe`); `ism.py` gives it the raw samples through a local rtl_tcp server and reads its messages. Licences and sources are in `installer\ISM.txt` (`lib\ism\README.txt` in the package).
