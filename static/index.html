HFDL (High Frequency Data Link): ACARS of aircraft over oceans and remote areas, plus logons, positions and frequency data, decoded by dumphfdl. Frequencies: 2.9–22 MHz, 16 ground stations, each on a few channels (the table below); the band buttons centre the VFO on a band. The audio of the VFO is muted while this decoder runs: all channels within ±(sample rate / 2) of the VFO centre are decoded at once, so a higher sample rate covers more of them (2.4 MS/s: the whole band, often 8.8 + 10 MHz or 10 + 11 MHz together). Needs HF: RTL-SDR Blog V4, a dongle with an upconverter (or direct sampling), LimeSDR, Airspy with an upconverter. Best by day on 8–22 MHz, by night on 3–11 MHz. Each ground station sends a squitter every 32 s on its active channels, so the heard stations show up even without aircraft traffic. ↑ = from the ground, ↓ = from the aircraft. Over the oceans aircraft are often out of ADS-B coverage: then "not seen on ADS-B now". Ground station: the one the message is addressed to (↓) or sent by (↑), from the HFDL frame header. A click on a message marks it and holds the list still (SCROLL PAUSED); the mouse wheel over the messages resumes it; a long message shows 3 lines, a click shows all. ADS-B and Mode S: the transponders of aircraft answer the radars and broadcast their identity, position, altitude and speed on 1090 MHz (pulse position modulation, 1 Mbit/s, 56 or 112 bits with a 24-bit CRC), decoded here in RADIMCOM (no external program). ON takes the VFO to 1090.000 MHz at 2.4 MS/s (an Airspy stays at 2.5 MS/s); the audio is muted and ACARS, VDL2, DAB, ISM, HFDL and AIS are turned off, because they need the dongle elsewhere. Turning ADS-B off gives the previous sample rate back. Messages: DF17 / DF18 ADS-B (flight, category, airborne and surface position, velocity, vertical rate, squawk and emergency, selected altitude and QNH), DF11 all-call replies and the radar replies DF0 / 4 / 5 / 16 / 20 / 21 (altitude, squawk) of the aircraft already heard. Positions are CPR-decoded from an even and an odd message (or from the last position); distance and azimuth from the station location (SETTINGS › GENERAL › LOCATION). One or a few wrong bits of a known aircraft are corrected with the CRC (counted as "fixed"). Range: line of sight, 100–300 km with a 1090 MHz antenna outdoors; a short whip indoors hears the nearest aircraft only. Country from the ICAO address; a click on the ICAO address opens globe.adsb.fi, on the flight adsb.lol, in a new tab. An aircraft not heard for 1 min is grey, after 10 min it is removed. RANGE (MAP) draws the farthest position decoded in each 2° sector from the station, for aircraft up to 10000 ft (green), 20000 ft (yellow), 30000 ft (orange) and at any altitude (violet); it is collected the whole time ADS-B runs, kept in data\adsb_range.json between restarts, and starts again when the station location moves. Only positions confirmed by an earlier position of the same aircraft count. CLR RANGE (twice) starts it again. Registration and type of each aircraft are looked up once on adsb.fi (adsb.lol when adsb.fi does not answer; needs internet); an aircraft heard here without a position of its own (radar replies only, or no position messages yet) gets the position adsb.fi sees, every 30 s: blue in the list and a blue aircraft on the map.

Each line gets a band-stop: flat over Width, sides as wide as Edge (far left = steepest), moved up or down by Shift (all lines together). AGC+
PRESETS

AGC+ key (GAIN): the tuner gain from the A/D level. On clipping it goes down at once, then waits settle blocks; it goes up only after hold without clipping and with the A/D peak under up below, aiming at target. One block ≈ 55–130 ms by the sample rate. Bold (up while clipping > 0): after each hold the gain goes one step up while the clipping stays at or under this many ppm, also with the peak at full scale; between that and clipping above it holds, above it goes down and then waits 4× hold. Keep clipping above ≥ 10× of it. PRESETS from careful to bold, the one matching the sliders is lit: CAREFUL = no clipping (the defaults before 0.114), MODERATE (default) = gain up to a −5 dBFS peak, short clipping up to 100 ppm tolerated, BOLD = climbs into clipping up to 20 ppm, down above 300 ppm. With WFM a block with a gain step is played in mono (the step would turn the stereo into noise). Smooth ALC gain (on by default, for this VFO): the audio ALC in AM/LSB/USB/CW raises its gain in a smooth ramp instead of steps, so it does not click and pump; it still falls at once. It is the audio gain after the demodulator, not the tuner gain (the tuner has only fixed steps).

Audio filters of the HPF and LPF keys, for this VFO. Faster: turn the mouse wheel over the key. HPF 20–5000 Hz, LPF 100–15000 Hz, 36 dB per octave. Together they make a band-pass, so HPF always stays below LPF. What you hear and REC are filtered, decoders are not. Not for DAB.

AFI – inversion frequency (Hz)

Audio frequency inversion (AFI key): the audio spectrum is mirrored around this frequency (f → 2·F − f, band 0 … 2·F). The same setting scrambles and descrambles. Default 1750 Hz (band up to 3500 Hz). 500–4000 Hz.

DNR – Dynamic Noise Reduction (DNR key)
Now

The DNR key (AUDIO, beside ALC; AM/LSB/USB/CW) switches on the ticked parts. EXP turns the noise between words down by the depth; speech stays at full level. A voice detector looks at the spectrum every 8 ms (speech has harmonics standing out of the noise, the noise is flat), opens at once on speech, holds a while and closes in 80 ms. In CW it watches only the CW passband.

AUTO (default): a noise blip lowers the sensitivity, missed weak speech raises it, a chop (reopening soon after closing) makes the hold longer (180–350 ms), the depth follows the measured SNR (8–18 dB). Settles in about 2–3 s, after a retune it starts again fast; Now shows the values in use. AUTO off: depth 3–30 dB (12), sensitivity 1–10 (5; chopped speech → raise, noise opening it → lower), hold 20–1000 ms (100; chopped words → longer). The SNR in Now (speech vs. noise between words) is measured in both modes.

LIM: soft limiter instead of hard clipping; with ALC +4 dB, the peaks rounded, not cut.

Auto Squelch ASQ – above the noise (dB)

ASQ key: the squelch level follows the noise in the channel plus this margin. The noise is measured in the spectrum of the dongle band (its quietest points, converted to the channel bandwidth), so a long transmission in the channel does not raise the level and close it. In a crowded band the quietest channel power of the last 15 s is used if it is lower. 2–30 dB, default 6 dB. With AGC+ on, a squelch set by the SQL bar of the VFO window works as auto squelch too (a fixed level would not fit the changing gain); the SQL bar then only turns it on and off.

Tone squelch in FM, keys TSQ-C (CTCSS, sub-audio tone 67.0–254.1 Hz) and TSQ-D (DCS, digital code at 134.4 bit/s). They add to the squelch of the SQL bar or ASQ: the audio passes only while that squelch is open and the set tone or code is received. Without the SQL bar / ASQ the tone alone opens it. One of them at a time. The tone is filtered out of what you hear (300 Hz high-pass). It opens about 0.3 s after the tone starts. Faster: turn the mouse wheel over the key. The VFO window shows the set tone and code under the frequency: grey = off, orange = on, lit orange box = received now. For this VFO.
